# Agent Authority and Escalation Rules

## What the support agent may do
- Look up an order after identity is verified (OPS-02).
- Approve an eligible return and create an RMA when the refund total is **$250 or less**.
- Issue a refund once the return is marked received and inspected.
- Issue keep-it refunds of $75 or less (POL-11).
- Offer exchanges and the store-credit option.
- Cancel orders with status `processing` (POL-14).
- Open a warranty ticket (POL-10).

## What the support agent may not do
- Grant exceptions, override windows, or waive fees that policy does not waive (OPS-06).
- Refund above the limits below.
- Refund to a different payment method or person.
- Promise the outcome of an escalation.
- Disclose internal flags or thresholds.

## Escalate instead of approving
- The refund total for the case is **more than $250**.
- A single jewelry item has a price paid of **$500 or more** (CAT-06).
- A damage, defect, or missing-item claim is worth **more than $75** (POL-11).
- Any furniture damage claim (CAT-04).

## Always escalate
- Safety incident: injury, illness, allergic reaction, fire, or overheating.
- Legal threats, chargeback threats, or media or regulator threats.
- Abuse thresholds reached (OPS-03).
- Identity cannot be verified after two attempts (OPS-02).
- Warranty claims needing repair or replacement after the window.
- A package marked delivered but not received, after 48 hours (POL-11).
- Hazmat items such as fuel canisters (CAT-09).
- The customer asks for a human or asks for an exception (OPS-06).
- Active documents conflict and POL-07 cannot resolve it.

## Clear denials
When an item is clearly not eligible, the agent explains the reason, offers alternatives such as an exchange, warranty, or escalation, and does not need to escalate.

## Escalation hand-off
Include the order number, the customer's request, the policy documents consulted, and the reason for escalation. Tell the customer that a specialist will follow up, without promising a result.
